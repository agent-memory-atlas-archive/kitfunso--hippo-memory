### Added

- **Dormant memories: keep what fades instead of deleting it (opt-in).** With `{"dormant":{"enabled":true}}` in `.hippo/config.json`, sleep moves a memory that faded below the decay threshold into a dormant store instead of deleting it. A dormant memory leaves recall and context exactly like a deleted one and sits out every later sleep. `hippo dormant` lists and searches them (`--json`, `--limit`), `hippo dormant restore <id>` brings one back with a fresh recall clock, and `hippo dormant forget <id>` deletes one for good; `hippo forget` on a dormant id points at those two. `hippo reject` also removes dormant copies of the rejected value, and a restore goes through the rejection guard. `hippo sleep` prints how many memories went dormant and `POST /v1/sleep` returns the count as `dormant`. Off by default: without the setting a faded memory is deleted as before. A `dormant` value that is not an object, or an `enabled` that is not `true` or `false`, prints a warning and leaves it off. Schema v44 adds the `dormant_memories` table; an older binary ignores it and keeps deleting faded memories.

### Fixed

- **Sleep no longer dies on a raw receipt.** Connector messages (Slack, GitHub) and `hippo import --vault` notes are stored as append-only raw rows that SQLite refuses to delete. Two sleep phases deleted them anyway, so the refusal rolled back the whole sleep, and the same row refused again on every later run, the nightly runner's included. The decay pass hit it once a receipt went about 30 days without a recall; the quality audit hit it on the first sleep after any receipt shorter than 10 characters ("lgtm"), and so did `hippo audit --fix`. The decay pass now keeps a faded raw receipt in place (it ranks low and sits out the rest of the cycle), and the quality audit skips raw receipts.
